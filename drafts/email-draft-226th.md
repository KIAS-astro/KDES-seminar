Title: MAUVE-MUSE: The Origin of the SFR–Metallicity Correlation
Speaker: Rongjun Huang, ICRAR, University of Western Australia
Abstract: On resolved scales, gas-phase metallicity is known to depend not only on stellar mass surface density, but also on local star formation activity. However, the physical origin of this secondary dependence remains debated, and most previous studies have been limited to kiloparsec-scale resolution. In this talk, I will present results from MAUVE-MUSE, which resolves late-type Virgo galaxies down to ∼100 pc scales and allows us to probe the interplay between local stellar mass, star formation, and chemical enrichment in unprecedented detail. I show that the metallicity–SFR coupling is not monotonic: at low Σ⋆, regions with enhanced star formation tend to be metal-poor, whereas at high Σ⋆, they tend to be metal-rich. The transition occurs around log(Σ⋆/M⊙ kpc^−2)∼7.5–8.0, and correlated and anti-correlated H II regions can coexist within the same galaxy. To interpret this behaviour, I introduce a spatially resolved gas-regulator framework based on gas and metal continuity equations, and show that the observed sign change can be understood as a transition between gas-supply/dilution-dominated variability and star-formation/enrichment-dominated variability.

Title: A Tomographic Alcock–Paczyński Test of Dynamical Dark Energy with the DESI DR2 BGS and LRGs
Speaker: Minseong Kwon, Seoul National University
Abstract: The apparent shape of cosmic structure depends on the cosmology assumed to convert observed redshifts into comoving distances. The Alcock–Paczyński (AP) test exploits this apparent distortion to constrain the expansion history. We use the two-point correlation function (2PCF) as a standard shape and measure its differential evolution with redshift. We apply this method to the DESI DR2 Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) and Luminous Red Galaxy (LRG) samples, covering 0.025 < z < 0.85. The systematics that affect the redshift evolution of the 2PCF shape (i.e., redshift-space distortions, survey geometry, and galaxy bias) are estimated with mock catalogs from Horizon Run 4, and their cosmology dependence is emulated using the Multiverse simulation suite. We have improved the methodology of the extended AP test and the likelihood estimation to constrain the dark energy equation of state based on the flat w^CPL CDM model. I will present its ongoing status in this talk.
